Edge construction: draw every coral branch, sea-grass blade, sponge, shell, starfish, seahorse, bubble, and sandy-base shape with a continuous fully closed contour and no gaps. Use colored outlines / line art matching the local object color (also called a color hold): each contour is a slightly darker or more saturated version of that object's adjacent fill color, never a generic black outline and never white. Maintain a visible colored contour even on the palest highlights and at every branch tip. Adjacent overlapping objects must remain visually separable with their own closed colored contours. No painted shape may fade into the background.

Style guard: keep the soft, translucent, airy watercolor character of the reference exactly. The contours are darkened watercolor pigment settling at the edge of each wash (like a wet-on-dry hard edge), NOT pen or ink lines. Do not increase color saturation or density beyond the reference. Do not turn the artwork into a coloring-book or lineart drawing.

White separation: the background alone may use absolute pure white #FFFFFF. Any light or white-looking area inside the actual illustration must be a visibly tinted off-white (pale blue-white, cream, blush, peach, lavender) enclosed by its object's colored contour. Do not use pure white pixels inside the subject.

Background: perfectly uniform solid pure white #FFFFFF, every background pixel the same, no paper texture, no wash, no gradient, no vignette, no shadow, no glow, no aura, no halo, no mist, and no color spill. The white background must begin immediately outside the colored subject contour.
