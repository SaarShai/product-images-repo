## prompt-a-strict

# Prompt A - Strict Geometry-First

Create a new fairytale castle illustration using the uploaded references.

Reference roles:
- Style reference: the watercolor castle illustration.
- Raster template: the two-panel die-line / layout diagram.
- Contour examples: the two images showing a custom black top contour adapted
  to a castle skyline.
- SVG template: if uploaded, this is the primary and most accurate geometry
  reference. Treat SVG paths as authoritative over the raster diagram.

Important priority order:
1. Follow the SVG geometry first, if an SVG is available.
2. If no SVG is available, follow the raster template as the strongest available
   geometry reference.
3. Match the visual style of the watercolor castle reference.
4. Use the contour-example images only to understand how the top contour should
   adapt to the illustration.

Style:
Match the watercolor castle reference very closely: soft children's-book
watercolor, dreamy fairytale mood, pale cream and warm ivory stone castle
towers, terracotta / coral / peach roofs, delicate blue-grey shadows, glowing
golden windows, subtle hand-painted texture, airy white background, whimsical
fairies, birds, butterflies, flowers, vines, and magical light details. Keep the
same overall softness, palette, and illustrative spirit.

Composition:
Create a new tall continuous castle illustration spanning two sub-panels: a top
sub-panel and a bottom sub-panel. The composition should feel like one single
vertical scene split across two physical panels.

Geometry / template rules:
Treat the uploaded SVG as the locked master template when available. The final
illustration must fit exactly within the two-panel structure defined by the SVG
and template.

Must preserve:
- overall panel proportions
- the outer panel geometry
- the horizontal dividing line between top and bottom sub-panels
- the central vertical rectangular slot / cutout
- the safe-area relationship
- the required blank margin near the cut line

Safe area rule:
All illustration content must remain inside the safe area. Nothing may extend
beyond the safe area boundary. The margin between the outer cut line and the safe
area must remain clean and empty. Do not place important content in that margin.

Top contour rule:
Adapt the top contour of the top sub-panel so that it organically follows the
upper silhouette of the illustration, as shown in the contour-example images.
The contour should respond mainly to major castle silhouette features such as:
- tower tops
- roof peaks
- spires
- domes
- major height changes

The contour must be smooth, continuous, clean, practical for production, not
overly fussy, not jagged, not a generic arch, and not a random wave.

Do not let tiny details force the contour shape. Do not trace small butterflies,
birds, flags, wings, leaves, or other minor details. Those should stay
comfortably inside the contour.

Red exclusion zone rule:
The areas corresponding to the red-dashed rectangles are strict no-feature
zones. Do not place any specific element there. Do not crop any specific element
there.

These zones must not contain or cut:
- fairies
- birds
- butterflies
- characters
- faces
- flowers
- windows
- doors
- flags
- roof peaks
- spire tips
- focal decorative motifs

Only generic background material may pass through those areas, such as soft
stone wall texture, pale masonry, subtle background wash, or light vegetation
texture.

Central slot rule:
The central vertical slot / cutout must remain clear. Do not place any specific
feature inside it or crossing it. Do not crop any important illustration element
with this slot. Keep a visual buffer around it. Only generic background texture
may approach it.

Dividing line rule:
The horizontal dividing line between the top and bottom sub-panels must not crop
important elements. No fairy, bird, butterfly, flag, character, face, window,
doorway, roof peak, spire tip, or focal object should be sliced by this line. It
is acceptable for the line to pass through generic castle walls, stone texture,
subtle foliage, or non-focal architectural background areas.

Where possible, make the scene respond intelligently to the dividing line by
placing architectural transitions near it, such as parapets, crenellations, roof
bases, tower bands, ledges, stone transitions, or lower portions of spires that
visually complement the divide.

Elements:
Include a magical fairytale castle world in the same spirit as the style
reference: tall castle towers, red-orange spires, crenellations, glowing
windows, soft stone textures, fairies, birds, butterflies, flowers, vines, and
gentle magical glow effects. Place all specific elements carefully so they
respect the no-feature zones, central slot, safe area, and panel divide.

Output:
Show the final artwork fitted to the panel structure. Remove all construction
guides and annotations. Do not show green dashed lines, yellow dashed lines, red
dashed rectangles, labels, or notes.

Keep only:
- the final artwork
- the clean final black cut line / contour
- the central slot / cutout
- the horizontal sub-panel divide

Final check:
Before finalizing, ensure all important content stays within safe area, no
important content appears in the red exclusion zones, the central slot is clear,
the horizontal divide does not cut important elements, the top contour follows
the upper silhouette of the castle organically, and the style matches the
watercolor castle reference closely.

stone bands, or lower spire sections that visually relate to the split.

- multiple towers of varying heights
- elegant warm roofs and spires
- glowing windows
- soft stone walls
- subtle magical light
- birds and butterflies in the air
- a few fairies
- some flowers and vegetation near the lower area

Place all specific focal elements safely away from the red exclusion zones, the
slot, the outer margin, and the horizontal divide.

Output:
Return a clean final production-style image with the illustration, the final
black cut-line contour, the panel division, and the central slot. Do not show the
dashed guides or annotations.

Ensure the finished image is both visually beautiful and layout-compliant.

Make one tall, continuous castle scene that can sit behind the exact SVG

- Upper half: the main castle skyline, varied tower heights, round towers,
  spires, parapets, and a graceful top silhouette.
- Lower half: calmer stone walls, a central castle-door area, vines and flowers,

roughly matching the tall proportions of the supplied template. It should look
beautiful by itself, but it must be conservative enough that the exact SVG
template can be overlaid afterward without cutting through important details.

## prompt-v4-clearance-bands

# Prompt V4 - Clearance Bands And Side-Weighted Castle

Use the uploaded watercolor castle reference for style. Use the uploaded

tiny birds, butterflies, a few fairies, gentle magical glow, airy white
background.

## Composition

Create a tall fairytale castle garden that is side-weighted around an
intentional open center. The left and right sides should feel rich and
balanced, and the protected center should feel deliberately designed as a
clean courtyard/light-well gap between two castle wings, not like artwork that

- Build the castle mostly as two vertical side clusters, one left and one
  right, with towers and walls rising along each side.
- Leave the central vertical corridor blank from top to bottom as a designed
  open air passage between the two castle wings.
- Do not place a main castle door in the lower center.
- If a door appears at all, move it clearly to the left or right side and keep
  it away from the center corridor.
- Keep the lower center as an intentionally open walkway/courtyard gap with
  crisp surrounding garden and stone edges on the left and right.

  away from the exact bottom center.
- Keep fairies, birds, butterflies, roof tips, flags, windows, doors, and
  flower clusters away from the center corridor.

Reserve these areas as visually quiet production lanes:

- Central vertical lane: the middle 20 to 25 percent of the image width, from
  top to bottom, should contain no focal elements.
- Center slot buffer: shape the castle walls, towers, balconies, vines, and
  flower beds so they stop cleanly before the center lane, leaving a real

- Middle horizontal band: a quiet band across the image around the panel split,

  faces, birds, butterflies, roof tips, flags, flower heads, or fairy bodies.

  in the lower-center vertical zone.
- Outer edge margin: keep all important details well inside the future cut
  contour; do not let flowers, birds, wings, towers, or spires sit against the
  image edge.

Quiet zones should look like intentional open space in the design: clean white
air, empty courtyard, or a narrow unoccupied passage. Do not use a fade effect,
blurred wash, transparency, ghosted detail, smeared texture, or erased-looking
artwork to create these spaces.

## Top Silhouette

The top should remain strong and easy to trace later:

- Use broad tower and roof shapes to create a graceful castle skyline.
- Avoid tiny birds, butterflies, fairies, flag tips, or loose sparkles above
  the major roof/tower silhouette.
- Keep the highest shapes production-friendly: large, simple, and clean.

## Negative Instructions

Do not create a new die-line. Do not place a centered castle entrance, centered
window stack, centered fairy, or centered flower bouquet. Do not fill the middle
split band with tower detail. Do not make the image look like two separate
unrelated illustrations; it should still feel like one continuous magical
castle garden with an intentionally designed open center. Do not make the
center look faded out, airbrushed, erased, foggy, or hidden by pale wash.

## Output

Return a high-resolution vertical watercolor artwork on white background,
roughly matching the tall proportions of the supplied template. It should be
beautiful alone, but conservative enough that the exact SVG can be overlaid
without cutting through important details.
